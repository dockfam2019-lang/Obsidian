#### Exchange 1

**User**

````text
How much can companies manipulate they’re reviews
````

**Assistant**

````text
**Quite a bit—especially the overall star rating.** A company with 4.8 stars isn’t automatically trustworthy.

Companies can influence reviews by:

- **Asking mostly happy customers** to leave reviews, which skews the sample.
- **Offering refunds or fixes** and asking dissatisfied customers to update their review.
- **Reporting negative reviews** for rule violations. The platform decides whether to remove them; the company usually can’t simply delete them.
- **Buying fake reviews or using employees and friends**, despite platform rules.
- **Controlling testimonials on their own website**, where they can choose exactly what appears.

**Look at recent 1–3-star reviews and repeated complaints**, especially about refunds, delays, or unfinished work. Detailed reviews spread over time generally tell you more than the headline rating.

Are you asking about Google reviews, Yelp, or reviews on a company’s website?
````

#### Exchange 2

**User**

````text
So you can just have people leave fake reviews
````

**Assistant**

````text
**Yes, companies can get people to post fake reviews, and some do.** A posted review isn’t proof that the person was actually a customer.

But in the U.S., businesses creating or buying fake reviews can face FTC enforcement and fines. Paying people specifically for positive reviews is also prohibited. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

Asking real customers to share their honest experience is the legitimate way to build reviews.
````

#### Exchange 3

**User**

````text
Why doeeent google say hey pay me and I’ll take down a bad review
````

**Assistant**

````text
Because **Google would be selling companies control over their reputation**, which would make its reviews much less trustworthy.

Bad businesses could pay to hide complaints, customers would stop trusting the ratings, and honest businesses would feel pressured to pay just to compete.

Google’s business benefits from people trusting its search results. Taking money to erase legitimate criticism could damage that trust—and create legal problems if it misled consumers.
````

#### Exchange 4

**User**

````text
Right but it brings them profit
````

**Assistant**

````text
Yes—**it could bring them profit in the short term.** Your point is valid.

But the question is whether that money would outweigh what they could lose. Google makes money from people using its services and advertisers paying to reach them. If reviews became known as something businesses could pay to erase, they’d become less useful and trusted.

A company can make money from one practice while that practice hurts its overall business.
````
