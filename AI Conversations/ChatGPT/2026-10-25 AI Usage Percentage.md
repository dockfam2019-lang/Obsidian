If you mean **AI tools like ChatGPT**, roughly **half of American adults use them at least occasionally**.

- **24%** use one daily.
- **44%** use ChatGPT specifically. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

**Worldwide, it’s lower:** Microsoft estimated **17.8% of working-age people** used generative AI in early 2026. That uses a different measurement, so it isn’t directly comparable to the U.S. survey. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

#### Exchange 2

**User**

````text
Okay so 24 percent use daily what percentage of people are geniunly doing highly advanced things thing with it like organizing their calendar emails having it do mass text outreach vibe coding
````

**Assistant**

````text
**A smaller minority—but there isn’t a reliable percentage for that exact level of use.** Daily use could simply mean asking questions or getting homework help.

The closest measurement I found: a July 2026 Menlo Ventures/Morning Consult survey found **24% of AI users regularly use an AI agent**—software that performs tasks and takes actions. Since 64% of adults in that survey used AI, that works out to **about 15% of U.S. adults.** :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

But **15% doesn’t mean they’re all running sophisticated workflows**. Using one agent regularly could mean a single narrow task. The percentage consistently combining email/calendar management, automated outreach, and building working apps **isn’t established by that survey**.

So yes: **getting AI to reliably handle substantial work puts you in a smaller group than simply using it daily.** I wouldn’t give you a “top 1%” or “top 5%” claim without evidence.
````
