**Codex response(s)**

````text
There’s a real product idea here: **a trusted payment-history network for contractors**. A contractor who avoids one costly nonpayment could see real value in it.

The hard part will be making the information trustworthy and fair. Build each report around a specific job and verifiable records: the signed estimate, approved changes, invoice, payment dates, and whether the amount is disputed. Give homeowners a way to respond or correct a record, and keep addresses and other identifying details private. Clear labels such as “invoice unpaid” are more useful than an unverified accusation like “scammer.”

There’s also a legal question to resolve before you share homeowner reports. The FCRA covers some services that assemble information about people for other businesses to use in decisions; federal law also includes a legitimate-business-need purpose connected to a transaction initiated by a consumer. Whether your particular service falls under those rules depends on its design, so have a lawyer who knows consumer-reporting law review it before launch. ([FTC overview](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/fair-credit-reporting-act), [15 U.S.C. § 1681b](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t15/s1681b/))

A practical first step is to interview contractors about their last few payment disputes: what happened, what proof they kept, and whether they already warn other contractors. That will tell you whether the best starting product is the shared reporting network, or a tool that helps contractors document jobs and prevent payment problems from the start.
````

````text
Got it. You want an idea that could change how people do something, not just make the current process a little smoother.

The strongest big idea I see is growing out of your contractor thought:

### A trust-and-payment system for home projects

Every job gets one shared record: agreed scope, photos before work, approved changes, milestones, payments, and how each side handled the project. Homeowners and contractors both build a track record from verified jobs.

The bigger opportunity is making that the normal way home repairs and renovations get done: fewer “he said, she said” disputes, clearer expectations, and more confidence hiring someone. Contractors could subscribe for the job-management tools; a homeowner marketplace or payment features could come later. I’d start with one trade in one city and test whether contractors would use the shared job record.

Two other big swings:

- **An AI that handles one painful life process end to end**—for example, managing an insurance claim, from organizing documents to following up and preparing each response for approval. A generic personal AI is already a crowded direction; major products are adding memory and multi-step actions, so the opening would be to own one process deeply. ([OpenAI on memory](https://openai.com/index/memory-and-new-controls-for-chatgpt/), [Notion on agents](https://www.notion.com/releases/2025-09-18))
- **A proof-based path into skilled work**—people learn through realistic practice, show what they can actually do, and carry verified work samples and skills to employers. That could give people a way into good jobs without relying only on degrees or résumés.

My instinct is to explore the home-project idea first. It came from a specific frustration you noticed, and it has room to grow into something much larger than reviews.
````
